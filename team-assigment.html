The initiative
Problem: over-engineering and complexity
Solution: Customer Delight

Simpler. Faster. More agile.

Why it works: easy products win tenders, ease partner work and fit a software-led model.

(45s) Argue the problem: products and processes are over-engineered, so they are slow to ship and hard to install. Alternatives such as a price war lose against Asian makers. Customer Delight competes on simplicity instead. The next slides test this with the models.
Strategy model 1

PESTEL

Political

Governments restrict Chinese vendors, so trusted Western brands gain tenders.

HIGH

Economic

Asian price pressure squeezes hardware margins; buyers move to subscriptions.

HIGH

Social

Safety demand grows, but so do privacy concerns about video analytics.

MEDIUM

Technological

Edge AI and cloud video turn cameras into software platforms.

HIGH

Environmental

Energy-efficiency and e-waste rules favour durable, long-warranty devices.

LOW

Legal

GDPR, EU AI Act, NIS2 and CRA raise compliance costs but reward secure vendors.

MEDIUM

So what: Politics, Economics and Technology matter most. Hardware loses value, trust and software gain it.

Impact rating: team judgement. Sources: EU AI Act and NIS2 texts; public trusted-vendor rules (e.g. US NDAA s.889).
(75s) Do not read every box. Focus on the three high-impact factors. Political: trusted-vendor rules open doors. Economic: hardware prices fall. Technological: value moves to analytics and cloud. Legal and social factors reward a vendor that is secure and simple.
Strategy model 2

Porter's Five Forces

Rivalry

Axis, Hanwha, Hikvision and Genetec compete on price and features.

HIGH

Buyer power

Tenders and integrators compare offers and switch easily (open standards).

HIGH

New entrants

Asian OEMs and AI start-ups enter, but scale and trust are barriers.

MEDIUM

Suppliers

Few chip and sensor vendors set terms.

MEDIUM

Substitutes

Radar, access control and cloud services replace some cameras.

MEDIUM

Verdict

Industry attractiveness: low to medium. Price alone cannot win; ease of use can.

Ratings: team judgement from the competitive landscape; competitor names are indicative.
(75s) Two forces are high: rivalry and buyers. Buyers can compare and switch, so we cannot rely on price or specifications. Substitutes are medium, not low, because cloud and sensor-based security can replace some cameras. Conclusion: differentiate on simplicity and partner experience.
Strategy model 3

McKinsey 7S

Strategy

Customer Delight, software and AI.

Shared values

Intelligence-first. Quality, security, delight.

Gap: Bosch-era complexity remains.

Style

From engineering-led to customer-led.

Structure

Standalone, fewer decision layers.

Staff

Keep talent; hire cloud and AI.

Systems

New IT, partner portal, simple quoting.

Skills

Strong hardware; gap in SaaS.

Hard S's (strategy, structure, systems) already point to Customer Delight. Soft S's (style, staff, skills) still carry Bosch-era habits. Fix the soft S's first.

Source: team analysis of the Bosch carve-out; no internal data available.
(60s) The shared values are right, but the gap is between hard and soft elements. Structure and systems can be changed by management decision. Style, staff and skills take longer and decide whether Customer Delight is real.

Recommendation

Roadmap and KPIs

0-6 months: Simplify

Cut product variants, simplify quoting and installation, launch the partner portal.

KPI: install time, quote time

6-18 months: Build

Hire cloud and AI talent, launch cloud VSaaS with partners, set up customer success.

KPI: NPS, partner satisfaction

18+ months: Scale

Shift mix to subscriptions, grow in trusted-vendor tenders, phase out legacy analog.

KPI: recurring revenue share
KPI targets to be set with management; baselines are not available to the team.
(60s) Three phases in order of risk. First fix what is cheap and visible to customers. Then build capabilities. Then scale subscriptions. Each phase has one KPI so the board can see progress.
